A record theft in about twenty minutes

On the evening of Thursday 29 October 2026, an attacker moved approximately USD 6.36 billion out of Binance wallets on ten blockchains, most of it in about 20 minutes starting at 20:05 UTC, according to an analysis published by the blockchain intelligence firm TRM Labs[1]. Binance estimated the financial impact of the unauthorised withdrawals at about $6.3 billion[2]. TRM describes the incident as the largest crypto theft on record and about four times the size of the $1.5 billion Bybit hack[1].

Binance suspended deposits and withdrawals, said that user funds were safe, and pledged to cover all losses from its emergency fund, known as SAFU (Secure Asset Fund for Users), and from company funds[2,3]. Deposits later reopened while withdrawals stayed suspended, and Decrypt reported that withdrawals were expected to resume in 45 hours, while Reuters reported a target of 48 hours[4,5].

How the attacker reached the funds

Binance's first explanation was that its "initial investigation suggests that the security incident may involve a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop, which allowed an attacker to gain access to a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions"[6]. The company said it had found "no evidence of any wallet private key leak"[7]. In a later update, Binance's co-chief executive Richard Teng wrote that the attacker "did not simply use one engineer's permissions, and this was not a 'one-click' action", and that the investigation had found additional points of breach[5,8].

TRM reconstructed the theft from public blockchain records[1]. On Ethereum, at 20:20 UTC, the operator submitted a single transaction containing 50 authorisations, each signed by a Binance hot wallet and each pointing that wallet to a "sweeper" contract that accepts instructions only from the operator[1]. On Bitcoin, Tron, Solana, the XRP Ledger and several other networks, Binance wallets sent funds to the attacker through standard signed transfers[1].

The Ethereum technique relied on EIP-7702, a feature introduced in Ethereum's Pectra upgrade[1]. It lets an ordinary wallet sign an authorisation that makes it run the code of a chosen smart contract, and each authorisation carries a nonce that must match the wallet's current count[9]. TRM explains that because Binance's hot wallet processes a steady flow of withdrawals, its count keeps rising, so preparing signatures for 50 consecutive counts made it certain that one of them would still be valid[1]. Kok Tin Gan of the security firm NyxLab said the attack appeared to involve "one signature that gave the attacker a never-expiring, full-access token"[7].

The case for attributing the theft to TraderTraitor

TRM and Chainalysis have both attributed the theft to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state hacking unit that the FBI blamed for the Bybit theft in February 2025[1,7]. TRM said it made the attribution "based on robust on and off-chain intelligence" and with high confidence[6]. Its published evidence is that the attacker's operator wallet received three small withdrawals from Tornado Cash, starting on the morning of 28 October[1]. TRM also points to staging that began well before the theft and to signatures obtained in advance and then used in one burst, features it says match the Bybit hack and the KelpDAO hack of April 2026[1]. Chainalysis co-founder Jonathan Levin said the attack was "likely done by DPRK group TraderTraitor"[7].

Elliptic said some of the stolen funds appeared in wallets used in the attack on the exchange Bitget earlier in 2026[10]. Chainalysis reported stolen funds "commingling in real time" with proceeds of the Bitget exploit[11]. Teng said the company had not attributed responsibility to any individual[8]. Reuters reported that North Korea routinely denies such allegations and that its mission in Geneva and embassy in China did not immediately respond to requests for comment[4].

Cryptocurrency theft and North Korean state revenue

In March 2024 the UN Panel of Experts reported that it was investigating 58 cyberattacks on cryptocurrency services between 2017 and 2023, worth about $3 billion, and assessed that such theft accounted for up to half of North Korea's foreign income[12]. On 28 March 2024, Russia vetoed the renewal of the panel's mandate in a Security Council vote of 13 in favour, one against and one abstention, by China[13,14]. Eleven governments, including the United States, Japan, South Korea, the United Kingdom and France, then formed the Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team to fill the monitoring gap left by the panel's disbandment[14,15]. Its report, released in October 2025, found that North Korean cyber actors stole at least $2.84 billion in cryptocurrency over 21 months[16].

Chainalysis found that North Korea-linked hackers stole $2.02 billion in 2025, a 51 per cent rise on the previous year, out of more than $3.4 billion stolen across the industry[17]. TRM calculated that North Korea took $577 million, or 76 per cent of global crypto hack losses, in the first four months of 2026[18]. TRM's Ari Redbord said: "When North Korea steals at this scale, the money funds weapons proliferation and other destabilising activity"[8].

Freezes, laundering and what remains unresolved

TRM counts USD 2.7 billion frozen, with Tether freezing 1.77 billion USDT from 21:01 UTC and Circle freezing 823 million USDC at 21:04 UTC[1]. Binance's estimate of about $3 billion frozen is higher than TRM's[1,2].

According to TRM, about 325 million USDT was sold to market makers through UniswapX and similar contracts for about 122,300 ETH, at more than a 10 per cent discount to market prices[1]. TRM estimates the attacker now controls about USD 2.4 billion beyond any issuer's reach, including more than 420,000 ETH and 18,270 BTC, and expects the next stage to involve cross-chain bridges, swap services and over-the-counter brokers[1]. Binance set aside about $6.34 billion of company funds to cover the losses, according to Reuters[4]. Sean Farrell of Fundstrat said the incident did not at present indicate a solvency problem, provided the frozen stablecoins were returned and withdrawals resumed in an orderly way[19].

Binance has not published a root-cause analysis, and its own account changed within a day from a single compromised laptop to several points of breach[1,5]. The attribution to North Korea comes from commercial firms, and the off-chain intelligence TRM relies on has not been published[1,6]. The amount frozen is about $3 billion according to Binance and USD 2.7 billion according to TRM[1,2]. It is also unclear how much of the roughly USD 2.4 billion outside issuers' reach can still be recovered[1].

1Twenty minutes that emptied the exchange's hot wallets

On the evening of Thursday 29 October 2026, an attacker moved approximately USD 6.36 billion out of Binance wallets on ten blockchains, most of it in about 20 minutes starting at 20:05 UTC, according to an analysis published by the blockchain intelligence firm TRM Labs[1]. Binance, which Reuters describes as the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange, estimated the financial impact of the unauthorised withdrawals at about $6.3 billion[2,8]. TRM describes the incident as the largest crypto theft on record and about four times the size of the $1.5 billion Bybit hack[1]. The British analytics firm Elliptic told Reuters that the incident "is almost certainly the single largest known theft of any kind"[8].

The headline figures differ slightly because they were measured at different moments and by different parties. TRM valued the stolen assets at USD 6.36 billion at the time they were moved and at USD 6.23 billion when it wrote its analysis, as cryptocurrency prices changed[1]. Binance's own figure of about $6.3 billion is the company's estimate of the impact[2]. Hot wallets, the target here, are wallets kept connected to the internet so that an exchange can process customer withdrawals quickly, which makes them more exposed than offline storage.

Binance suspended deposits and withdrawals, said that user funds were safe, and pledged to cover all losses from its emergency fund, known as SAFU (Secure Asset Fund for Users), and from company funds[2,9]. It said it had worked with blockchain partners to freeze approximately $3 billion of the affected assets[2]. Deposits later reopened while withdrawals stayed suspended, and Decrypt reported that withdrawals were expected to resume in 45 hours, while Reuters reported a target of 48 hours[6,8].

The story matters beyond one company because of who is suspected. TRM Labs and Chainalysis have both attributed the theft to TraderTraitor, a North Korean state hacking unit that the FBI blamed for the Bybit theft in February 2025[1,3]. Binance's co-chief executive Richard Teng said the company had not attributed responsibility to any individual[10]. For scale, Chainalysis attributed $2.02 billion in stolen cryptocurrency to North Korea-linked hackers across the whole of 2025, which was itself a record year[4]. If the attribution holds, a single operation would have brought in roughly three times that annual total.

2From an engineer's laptop to the signing system

Binance's first explanation was that its "initial investigation suggests that the security incident may involve a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop, which allowed an attacker to gain access to a service container and submit unauthorized withdrawal transactions"[7]. The company said it had found "no evidence of any wallet private key leak"[3]. Private keys are the secret codes that control a cryptocurrency wallet, so this statement implies that the attacker made Binance's own systems approve the transfers rather than stealing the keys. In a later update, Teng wrote that the attacker "did not simply use one engineer's permissions, and this was not a 'one-click' action", and that the investigation had found additional points of breach[6]. He promised "a detailed technical post-mortem once the investigation is complete and every fact is verified"[6].

The most detailed technical account so far comes from TRM, which reconstructed the theft from public blockchain records[1]. On Ethereum, at 20:20 UTC, the operator submitted a single transaction containing 50 authorisations, each signed by a Binance hot wallet and each pointing that wallet to a "sweeper" contract that accepts instructions only from the operator[1]. The first sweep, at 20:23:24 UTC, moved 167,464 ETH and 63 tokens, including 1.615 billion of the stablecoin USDT[1]. On Bitcoin, Tron, Solana, the XRP Ledger and several other networks, Binance wallets sent funds to the attacker through standard signed transfers[1]. Decrypt reported that this pattern suggested a compromise of Binance's hot wallet signing infrastructure[11].

The Ethereum technique relied on EIP-7702, a feature introduced in Ethereum's Pectra upgrade[1]. The specification, written by Vitalik Buterin, Sam Wilson, Ansgar Dietrichs and lightclient and now marked final, lets an ordinary wallet sign an authorisation that makes it run the code of a chosen smart contract[12]. Each authorisation carries a counter, or nonce, that must match the wallet's current count, a protection against the same signature being reused[12]. TRM explains that because Binance's hot wallet processes a steady flow of withdrawals, its count keeps rising, so preparing signatures for 50 consecutive counts made it certain that one of them would still be valid[1].

Independent specialists have offered partial interpretations. Kok Tin Gan of the security firm NyxLab said the attack appeared to involve "one signature that gave the attacker a never-expiring, full-access token"[3]. Mark Karpeles, quoted by Al Jazeera, suggested the attackers had accessed at least two wallet keys, which sits uneasily with Binance's statement[10]. TRM itself notes that Binance has yet to publish a root cause[1].

3How analysts traced the theft to TraderTraitor

The attribution to North Korea rests, for now, on the work of private companies. TRM said it attributed the attack to TraderTraitor "based on robust on and off-chain intelligence" and with high confidence[7]. Its published evidence is that the attacker's operator wallet received three small withdrawals from Tornado Cash, a service that hides where cryptocurrency comes from, starting on the morning of 28 October[1]. TRM also points to staging that began well before the theft and to signatures obtained in advance and then used in one burst, features it says match the Bybit hack and the KelpDAO hack of April 2026[1]. Chainalysis co-founder Jonathan Levin said the attack was "likely done by DPRK group TraderTraitor"[3].

A second line of evidence links the money to an earlier theft. Elliptic said some of the stolen funds appeared in wallets used in the attack on the exchange Bitget earlier in 2026[13]. Chainalysis reported stolen funds "commingling in real time" with proceeds of the Bitget exploit and described "never-seen-before laundering patterns"[11]. The "off-chain intelligence" that TRM mentions has not been published, so outside researchers cannot test that part of the case.

TraderTraitor is the name the FBI uses for the group, and news outlets often describe the unit as part of the wider Lazarus Group[10,14]. In a public service announcement dated 26 February 2025, the FBI said North Korea was responsible for stealing approximately $1.5 billion in virtual assets from Bybit on or about 21 February 2025 and attributed the attack to actors it calls TraderTraitor[14]. In December 2024, Japan's National Police Agency, working with the FBI and the US Defense Cyber Crime Center, named TraderTraitor as responsible for the theft of about 48.2 billion yen (about $308 million) from the Japanese exchange DMM Bitcoin[15]. The Bybit theft began when hackers compromised the computer of a developer at Safe, the company whose wallet software Bybit used[11].

North Korea's government has not responded publicly to the Binance accusations. Reuters reported that North Korea routinely denies such allegations and that its mission in Geneva and embassy in China did not immediately respond to requests for comment[8]. In May 2026, a foreign ministry spokesperson, speaking through the state news agency KCNA, called the idea of a North Korean cyber threat "nothing but an absurd slander to tarnish the image of our country" and warned of "all necessary measures for defending the interests of the state" in cyberspace[16].

4Cryptocurrency theft as a source of state revenue

In March 2024 the UN Panel of Experts, which monitored sanctions on North Korea, reported that it was investigating 58 cyberattacks on cryptocurrency services between 2017 and 2023, worth about $3 billion[5]. The panel assessed that such theft accounted for up to half of North Korea's foreign income[5]. That share is an estimate drawn from the cases the panel investigated, not an audited figure.

The panel no longer exists. On 28 March 2024, Russia vetoed the renewal of its mandate in a Security Council vote of 13 in favour, one against and one abstention, by China[17,18]. Russia's representative said "The Panel of Experts is now a weapon", and China's said "Sanctions should not be set in stone or be indefinite"[17]. The United States said Moscow had acted to hide "Russia's blatant violations of Security Council resolutions"[17].

Eleven governments, including the United States, Japan, South Korea, the United Kingdom and France, then formed the Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team (MSMT) to fill the monitoring gap left by the panel's disbandment[18,19]. Its report on cyber activity, released in October 2025, found that North Korean cyber actors stole at least $2.84 billion in cryptocurrency over 21 months[20]. A joint statement by the member states accused North Korea of exploiting foreign governments, businesses and the public "to steal and fraudulently obtain billions of dollars for its unlawful weapons of mass destruction and ballistic missile programs"[18].

The private data point the same way. Chainalysis found that North Korea-linked hackers stole $2.02 billion in 2025, a 51 per cent rise on the previous year, out of more than $3.4 billion stolen across the industry[4]. It found that North Korea managed this with 74 per cent fewer known attacks, and it estimates the cumulative total at $6.75 billion[4]. TRM calculated that North Korea took $577 million, or 76 per cent of global crypto hack losses, in the first four months of 2026, mainly through the Drift ($285 million) and KelpDAO ($292 million) attacks[21]. TRM's Ari Redbord said: "When North Korea steals at this scale, the money funds weapons proliferation and other destabilising activity"[10]. On 28 October 2026, the day before the Binance theft, the US Treasury sanctioned ten individuals and one entity over North Korean IT worker schemes[22].

5Freezes, reserves and a race against laundering

Much of the stolen value was held in stablecoins, tokens pegged to the US dollar and issued by companies that can freeze them. TRM counts USD 2.7 billion frozen[1]. In its count, Tether froze 1.77 billion USDT from 21:01 UTC and Circle froze 823 million USDC at 21:04 UTC[1]. Ripple froze 40.5 million RLUSD and World Liberty Financial froze 27.8 million USD1, while Arbitrum's Security Council froze assets worth about USD 30 million[1]. France 24 notes that World Liberty Financial is a Trump family venture[23]. Binance's estimate of about $3 billion frozen is higher than TRM's, and Reuters quoted Binance co-founder Yi He as saying that Ripple alone froze roughly $200 million, about five times TRM's figure[1,2,8].

The attacker moved quickly to sell assets that could be frozen. According to TRM, about 325 million USDT was sold to market makers through UniswapX and similar contracts for about 122,300 ETH, at more than a 10 per cent discount to market prices[1]. A second wallet deposited 60 million USDT with the lending protocol Aave and borrowed about 19,760 ETH against it, and 300 million USDT on Tron went to the JustLend protocol[1]. TRM estimates the attacker now controls about USD 2.4 billion beyond any issuer's reach, including more than 420,000 ETH and 18,270 BTC[1]. It expects the next stage to involve cross-chain bridges, swap services and over-the-counter brokers[1]. Chainalysis has documented that North Korean laundering usually follows a cycle of about 45 days[4].

Binance set aside about $6.34 billion of company funds to cover the losses, according to Reuters[8]. CoinDesk reported that $5 billion of this was already held in the affected tokens and that the rest was bought on public markets and through over-the-counter deals[24]. CoinDesk also reported that the SAFU fund usually holds about $1 billion in bitcoin[24]. A Binance spokesperson said "Ultimately, all user funds will be verified publicly through our Proof of Reserves"[24]. TechCrunch noted that Binance had not provided proof for its statement that user funds were safe[13].

Markets fell. Bitcoin dropped to a six-week low of $76,368 and ether fell more than 3 per cent to $2,178[8]. Shares in Coinbase fell 4.8 per cent, although that exchange said it was not affected[10]. Tim Sun of HashKey Group said the theft could "prompt investors to reassess the safety of assets held on centralised exchanges"[8]. Sean Farrell of Fundstrat said the incident did not at present indicate a solvency problem, provided the frozen stablecoins were returned and withdrawals resumed in an orderly way[25].

6An exchange with a contested record faces renewed scrutiny

Binance has been hacked before, on a much smaller scale. In May 2019 attackers took about 7,000 bitcoin, then worth about $40 million, using phishing, viruses and stolen API keys and two-factor codes, and Binance covered the loss from SAFU[26]. Its founder Changpeng Zhao called that episode a "very expensive lesson"[26].

The company's regulatory history is also relevant. On 21 November 2023, Binance pleaded guilty in the United States to conspiracy to violate the Bank Secrecy Act, failure to register as a money transmitting business and violation of the International Emergency Economic Powers Act[27]. It agreed to pay $4.3 billion and to retain an independent monitor[27]. Zhao pleaded guilty to failing to maintain an effective anti-money laundering programme, served four months in prison and stepped down as chief executive[27,28]. President Donald Trump pardoned him on 23 October 2025, and the White House press secretary described the prosecution as part of the previous administration's "war on cryptocurrency"[28]. Zhao remains Binance's largest shareholder[29]. After the theft he wrote: "As a shareholder, I expect all user funds will be fully covered"[9].

Binance said it was working with the FBI and the US Secret Service, and Abu Dhabi's Financial Services Regulatory Authority said it was "engaging closely with Binance and monitoring the incident"[8]. Teng called it "a complex attack" and said the company was coordinating with law enforcement[2]. Binance's chief security officer, Jimmy Su, had said in August 2025 that North Korean attackers try to get hired at Binance every day and called them "the single biggest threat to crypto companies"[11].

Critics have focused on internal controls. TechCrunch asked why a single employee's laptop could reach systems controlling billions of dollars, and whether Binance had commissioned third-party security audits before the breach[13]. The Bitget case a month earlier suggests a common pattern. On 24 September 2026, Bitget reported unauthorised transfers of about $351.6 million from its hot wallets and said its User Protection Fund, holding over $464 million, covered the loss[30]. Bitget later raised its estimate to $387.5 million and said the method was "highly consistent with known patterns of North Korean hacker organizations"[31]. At Bitget, attackers appear to have abused the system that authorises withdrawals rather than stealing keys outright, while TRM says the Binance transfers used signatures obtained in advance and executed in one burst[1,31].

Uncertainties and open questions

The cause of the breach is still unknown. Binance has not published a root-cause analysis, and its own account changed within a day from a single compromised laptop to several points of breach[1,6]. The attribution to North Korea comes from commercial firms, not from a government, and the off-chain intelligence TRM relies on has not been published[1,7]. Binance has not named an attacker[10]. North Korea did not respond to requests for comment and has rejected earlier accusations as slander[8,16].

Several figures disagree. The amount frozen is about $3 billion according to Binance and USD 2.7 billion according to TRM, and Ripple's share is 40.5 million RLUSD in TRM's count against roughly $200 million in the figure Reuters attributes to Yi He[1,2,8]. Even the Bybit benchmark varies: the FBI gives approximately $1.5 billion, while TechCrunch reports $1.4 billion[13,14]. Totals of North Korean theft also differ with the source and period covered: the UN panel counted about $3 billion from 2017, the MSMT at least $2.84 billion over 21 months, and Chainalysis $2.02 billion in 2025 alone[4,5,20]. It is also unclear how much of the roughly USD 2.4 billion outside issuers' reach can still be recovered[1].

Conclusion

Blockchain records show that about $6.3 billion left Binance's wallets in a short, carefully prepared burst on 29 October[1,2]. The firms that tracked the funds have attributed the theft with high confidence to North Korea's TraderTraitor unit, the group the FBI blamed for the Bybit theft[1,14]. However, that conclusion has not yet been confirmed by any government. About half the stolen value has been frozen, and Binance says it will cover the remaining losses from its own funds[2,8].

Three things are still to come: Binance's promised post-mortem, an updated proof of reserves, and any official attribution[6]. Since the UN panel was disbanded, monitoring of North Korea's sanctions evasion has been carried on by an eleven-government team set up to fill the gap[17,18,19]. That makes the work of commercial blockchain analysts more important to tracing these funds than it once was.
