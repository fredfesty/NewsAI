Withdrawals reopen after a record theft

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange by daily trading volume, began allowing customers to withdraw funds again on Sunday 1 November 2026, three days after attackers drained assets from its hot wallets[1]. Hot wallets are internet-connected wallets that an exchange uses to process frequent transactions, as distinct from cold storage[2]. The Bitcoin network was to reopen first, in a window between 06:00 and 09:00 GMT, with other networks following in phases[1].

The theft began at about 20:05 UTC on Thursday 29 October[3]. Binance put the loss at about $6.3bn of tokens, TRM Labs estimated USD 6.36bn, and the FBI referred to "more than $6 billion USD in virtual assets"[1,3,4]. TRM Labs describes the incident as the largest crypto theft on record and about four times the roughly $1.5bn taken from the exchange Bybit in February 2025[3]. All of these figures are dollar valuations of tokens at market prices, so they shift as prices move and should be read as approximate.

TRM Labs says the main wave of outflows ran from 20:05 to 20:22 UTC on several chains at once, after preparation that began at least 36 hours earlier[3]. By its estimate, about $3.79bn left on Ethereum, about $1.47bn on Bitcoin and about $628m on Tron[3]. These are TRM's own estimates rather than numbers published by Binance.

A compromised laptop and an unsettled technical cause

Binance's preliminary explanation was that a laptop belonging to a DevOps engineer had been compromised, giving the attacker access to a service container from which unauthorised withdrawal transactions were submitted[2,5]. The exchange said investigators had found no evidence that wallet private keys had leaked[2,5]. Teng later said investigators had found additional breach points beyond the laptop[6,7]. Safe Security, a cyber-risk company, notes that the exact authorisation pathway remains unverified[2].

TRM's technical account offers one explanation of how withdrawals could be authorised without stolen keys. On Ethereum, the firm says, the attacker used EIP-7702, an account-delegation feature, submitting 50 pre-signed authorisations that converted a Binance hot wallet into a sweeper contract under the attacker's control[2,3]. On the other chains TRM observed standard signed transfers, which it says indicates "compromise of Binance's hot wallet signing infrastructure"[3].

Attribution to North Korea and the dissenting voices

TRM Labs attributed the attack to TraderTraitor, which it calls "North Korea's elite hacking unit", adding: "We make this judgment with high confidence"[3,6]. It cites staging patterns matching the Bybit theft, funding traced through Tornado Cash, pre-signed batch authorisations and coordination across chains that suggests unified access to infrastructure[3]. Jonathan Levin, co-founder and chief executive of Chainalysis, said the hack was "likely done" by TraderTraitor[6].

On 31 October the FBI said North Korea was responsible for the theft of "more than $6 billion USD in virtual assets" from Binance[1,8]. The bureau, working with the US Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation, said TraderTraitor actors were converting stolen assets into Ethereum and that further laundering through decentralised finance (DeFi) and instant swap services was expected[4,8]. According to CoinDesk, the statement listed more than 600 Ethereum addresses, fewer than 20 Bitcoin addresses and one Solana address[8].

Not everyone has endorsed the attribution. Binance said: "Our investigation has not attributed responsibility to any individual"[9]. President Donald Trump said, "We don't know if it's North Korea", according to the Associated Press, and Fox Business reported him adding, "but if it is, we'll talk to them about it"[10,11]. In May 2026, responding to an earlier TRM Labs attribution, a North Korean Foreign Ministry spokesperson quoted by the state news agency KCNA called such allegations "absurd slander"[12].

North Korean theft and weakened sanctions monitoring

Chainalysis estimates that North Korean hackers stole $2.02bn in cryptocurrency during the whole of 2025, a record year, which was 51% more than in 2024 despite 74% fewer confirmed attacks[13]. Recorded Future News reported that this was 59% of the $3.4bn stolen worldwide that year, whereas TRM Labs gave a more conservative figure of over $1.5bn, a gap that NK News attributed to differing attribution methods[14,15]. In May 2023 the White House official Anne Neuberger said about half of North Korea's missile programme was financed by cyber theft[16].

For 15 years a UN Panel of Experts reported on how sanctions on North Korea's weapons programmes were implemented[17]. On 28 March 2024 Russia vetoed renewal of its mandate, in a vote of 13 in favour, Russia against and China abstaining[18]. To fill the gap, 11 states set up the Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team in October 2024, but Russia and China do not take part, and it does not operate under the Security Council mandate that the panel held[19,20].

Freezes, laundering and Binance's reserves

Binance said about $3bn of affected assets had been frozen[5,6]. Reuters reported that Tether froze nearly $1.57bn and Circle more than $800m, about $2.37bn combined, while CoinDesk put stablecoin freezes at about $2.4bn[8,21]. The differences probably reflect counts made at different times and with different scopes, and no audited single figure has been published.

TRM says the attackers converted about $325m of USDT to Ether through UniswapX at a discount of more than 10% and split Bitcoin through so-called peel chains[3]. "Ultimately, they're trying to off-ramp it to fiat. And that becomes the challenge, that's sort of this race against time," said Ari Redbord of TRM Labs[22].

Binance has said that "Binance will absorb the financial impact of the stolen funds, not our users"[1]. It says it has set aside more than $6.34bn of company resources to cover the losses, and its co-chief executive, Richard Teng, put replenishment at about 95% on 1 November, up from the 90% announced the previous day[1,23]. The National reported that Bitcoin fell 4.5% to $76,664 and Binance's own token BNB 7.1%, with more than $1.5bn of positions liquidated in 24 hours[5].

Several central facts remain uncertain. The attribution rests on the FBI's statement and on assessments by TRM Labs and Chainalysis, while Binance has not attributed responsibility and Trump has questioned the finding[6,9,10]. The replenishment percentages are Binance's own claims, and how much of the roughly $3bn not yet frozen can be recovered before it is converted into cash is unknown[7,22,23].

1Withdrawals reopen after the largest recorded crypto theft

The exchange said the Bitcoin network would reopen first, in a window between 06:00 and 09:00 GMT, with other networks following in phases[1]. Binance said it was "prioritizing tokens based on the past week data of user demand, so that each network and asset pair can be restored carefully, with stability and security checks completed before access is reopened"[1]. The trade publication crypto.news reported that 15 blockchain networks were in the first phase and that the exchange's founder, Changpeng Zhao, posted "BTC withdrawal is live"[5].

The theft began at about 20:05 UTC on Thursday 29 October[2]. Its size differs slightly by source: Binance put the loss at about $6.3bn of tokens, the blockchain intelligence firm TRM Labs estimated USD 6.36bn, and the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) referred to "more than $6 billion USD in virtual assets"[1,2,15]. Chainalysis, another blockchain analytics company, estimates that North Korean hackers stole $2.02bn in cryptocurrency during the whole of 2025, a record year[4]. If the FBI's attribution is correct, a single operation lasting minutes took roughly three times that annual total, which makes the event significant for sanctions enforcement as well as for the crypto industry. Binance has said that "Binance will absorb the financial impact of the stolen funds, not our users"[1]. Its co-chief executive, Richard Teng, said on 1 November that about 95% of the affected assets had been replenished, up from the 90% announced the previous day[5].

2Twenty minutes across ten blockchains

TRM Labs' reconstruction, published on 30 October, says the main wave of outflows ran from 20:05 to 20:22 UTC on several chains at once, followed by a second wave at around 23:05 UTC on the Avalanche and Polygon networks[2]. The firm says preparation began at least 36 hours earlier, including test deployments and withdrawals from the Tornado Cash mixing service on 28 October[2]. By its estimate, about $3.79bn left on Ethereum, about $1.47bn on Bitcoin, about $628m on Tron, about $296m on the XRP Ledger and about $96m on Solana, with smaller sums on Arbitrum, Optimism, Base, Avalanche and Polygon[2]. These per-chain figures are TRM's own estimates rather than numbers published by Binance.

Early public information was patchy. On 29 October TechCrunch reported estimates from crypto watchers ranging from at least $2bn to upward of $5.7bn, and quoted Binance as saying "Rest assured that user funds are completely safe"[16]. A Binance spokesperson, Ross O'Leary, said the exchange had "temporarily suspended withdrawals on multiple networks following a security incident as a precautionary measure"[16].

Binance's preliminary explanation was that a laptop belonging to a DevOps engineer, one of the staff who manage software deployment and infrastructure, had been compromised, giving the attacker access to a service container from which unauthorised withdrawal transactions were submitted[6,14]. The exchange said investigators had found no evidence that wallet private keys had leaked[6,14]. Teng later said investigators had found additional breach points beyond the laptop, and CBS News reported the same finding[7,17]. Safe Security, a cyber-risk company, notes that the exact authorisation pathway remains unverified[14].

TRM's technical account offers one explanation of how withdrawals could be authorised without stolen keys. On Ethereum, the firm says, the attacker used EIP-7702, an account-delegation feature, submitting 50 pre-signed authorisations that converted a Binance hot wallet into a sweeper contract under the attacker's control[2,14]. On the other chains TRM observed standard signed transfers, which it says indicates "compromise of Binance's hot wallet signing infrastructure"[2]. Safe Security found that the delegation transactions preceded the largest transfers by minutes and that assets moved within about 3 minutes and 24 seconds of a smart contract being deployed[14]. Its analyst Rahul Tyagi argues that "credential validity does not guarantee that an operation is legitimate"[14].

3How investigators traced the attack to North Korea

Attribution came first from private firms. TRM Labs attributed the attack to TraderTraitor, which it calls "North Korea's elite hacking unit", adding: "We make this judgment with high confidence"[2,7]. TRM cites staging patterns matching the Bybit theft, funding traced through Tornado Cash, pre-signed batch authorisations and coordination across chains that suggests unified access to infrastructure[2]. Jonathan Levin, co-founder and chief executive of Chainalysis, said the hack was "likely done" by TraderTraitor[7]. Outside researchers can check the on-chain movements the firms describe, but not any non-public intelligence behind their conclusions.

The FBI's statement followed on 31 October: "The Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) is releasing information advising the Democratic People's Republic of Korea (North Korea) was responsible for the theft of more than $6 billion USD in virtual assets from cryptocurrency exchange Binance, on or about October 29, 2026"[1,3]. The bureau, working with the US Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation, said TraderTraitor actors were converting stolen assets into Ethereum and that further laundering through decentralised finance (DeFi) and instant swap services was expected[3,15]. According to CoinDesk, the statement listed more than 600 Ethereum addresses, fewer than 20 Bitcoin addresses and one Solana address, and urged node operators, exchanges, DeFi services and analytics firms to block transactions linked to them[3]. The format follows the FBI alert of 26 February 2025, which named TraderTraitor as responsible for the Bybit theft and listed more than 50 Ethereum addresses[18].

The name has a longer history. An April 2022 advisory from the FBI, the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA) and the US Treasury described TraderTraitor as a family of trojanised cryptocurrency applications spread by spear-phishing, including fake recruitment offers aimed at DevOps staff and system administrators, and linked it to the North Korean state-sponsored group tracked as Lazarus Group, APT38 and BlueNoroff[19]. Chainalysis reported in December 2025 that DPRK-linked actors increasingly impersonate recruiters and investors, using fake hiring processes to harvest credentials and infrastructure access[4]. Binance's account of a compromised DevOps laptop fits that documented pattern, although a similarity in method is not proof of who carried out the attack.

Not everyone has endorsed the attribution. Binance itself said: "Our investigation has not attributed responsibility to any individual"[12]. President Donald Trump told reporters at a Halloween event at Mar-a-Lago, "We don't know if it's North Korea", according to the Associated Press, and Fox Business reported him adding, "but if it is, we'll talk to them about it"[20,21]. The technology site Tech Insider reported on 1 November that it had found no response from Pyongyang[22]. In May 2026, responding to an earlier TRM Labs attribution, a Foreign Ministry spokesperson quoted by the North Korean state news agency KCNA called such allegations "absurd slander" and a "political tool" of US hostility, according to the crypto news site Coin360[23].

4A long record of theft and a weakened sanctions watchdog

United Nations sanctions on North Korea, including an asset freeze, were imposed under Security Council resolution 1718, and for 15 years a UN Panel of Experts assessed how the Council's resolutions on the country were implemented[9,24]. According to PBS NewsHour, the panel's last report examined 58 suspected North Korean cyberattacks between 2017 and 2023 valued at about $3bn, funds that allegedly supported weapons of mass destruction development[10]. On 28 March 2024 Russia vetoed renewal of the panel's mandate, in a vote of 13 in favour, Russia against and China abstaining[10]. Russia's ambassador, Vassily Nebenzia, later told the General Assembly that an extension "would not contribute to normalising the situation on the Korean Peninsula"[25]. The United States, France, Japan, South Korea and the United Kingdom said jointly that "Russia's veto is nothing more than an attempt to silence independent, objective investigations into persistent violations"[24].

To fill the gap, 11 states, among them the US, Japan, South Korea, the UK, France and Germany, set up the Multilateral Sanctions Monitoring Team (MSMT) in October 2024[9]. Its second report, released on 22 October 2025, accused the DPRK of exploiting "foreign governments, private businesses, and the public to steal and fraudulently obtain billions of dollars" for "unlawful weapons of mass destruction and ballistic missile programs", and tied the activity to UN-designated entities, particularly the Reconnaissance General Bureau[9]. Joohui Park of South Korea's National Security Research Institute, writing for the Center for Strategic and International Studies (CSIS), put the thefts at about USD 1.19bn in 2024 and at least USD 1.645bn from January to September 2025[26]. The trade publication WorldECR reported the team's total as $2.84bn over 21 months[27]. Russia and China do not take part in the MSMT, which does not operate under the Security Council mandate that the UN panel held[9,11].

Private estimates vary. Chainalysis says North Korean hackers stole $2.02bn in 2025, 51% more than in 2024, despite 74% fewer confirmed attacks, bringing its all-time estimate to $6.75bn[4]. Recorded Future News reported that this was 59% of the $3.4bn stolen worldwide that year[28]. TRM Labs gave a more conservative figure of over $1.5bn out of $2.7bn, a gap that NK News attributed to differing attribution methods[29]. For the first half of 2026, the security firm Blockaid estimated North Korean thefts at about $609m, roughly 55% of verified exploit losses[30]. In May 2023 the White House official Anne Neuberger said about half of North Korea's missile programme was financed by cyber theft[31].

5Freezes, laundering and the race to cash out

Recovery so far has depended heavily on stablecoins, dollar-linked tokens that their issuers can freeze, unlike Bitcoin or Ether[8,13]. Binance said about $3bn of affected assets had been frozen[6,7]. Reuters reported that Tether froze nearly $1.57bn and Circle more than $800m, about $2.37bn combined[8]. TRM put the frozen total at about $2.7bn, listing $1.77bn in Tether's USDT, $825m in Circle's USDC, $40.5m in Ripple's RLUSD, $27.8m in World Liberty Financial's USD1 and $30m frozen by the Arbitrum Security Council[2]. CoinDesk put stablecoin freezes at about $2.4bn[3]. The differences probably reflect counts made at different times and with different scopes, and no audited single figure has been published.

US law enforcement describes the response as a joint effort with industry. The FBI and Secret Service said investigators "are working around the clock with our private-sector partners, including Circle, to identify, freeze, trace and recover funds stolen from Binance"[8]. After the Bybit theft, by contrast, The Register reported that only a little over $40m of about $1.5bn had been frozen by the time the FBI named North Korea[32].

The attackers moved quickly into assets that cannot be frozen. TRM says they converted about $325m of USDT to Ether through UniswapX at a discount of more than 10%, supplied $60m of USDT to the Aave lending protocol, moved $300m of USDT to JustLend on Tron and split Bitcoin through so-called peel chains[2]. Joe Hancock of the law firm Mishcon de Reya told CNBC: "The attackers knew that route would be closed to them and so got out ahead of that"[13]. Levin said the hackers "repeatedly circulate them among addresses, often sending funds back to addresses involved earlier in the sequence", and told CoinDesk that the "thousands of addresses" already in use could grow to "tens of thousands over the coming weeks"[3,13].

The end point is conversion into ordinary money. "Ultimately, they're trying to off-ramp it to fiat. And that becomes the challenge, that's sort of this race against time," said Ari Redbord of TRM Labs[13]. Redbord told the Associated Press it was "bank robbery, in this case, at the speed of the Internet", while noting that investigators can follow the funds in real time[20]. Chainalysis has documented that North Korean laundering typically unfolds over about 45 days, with funds split into tranches under $500,000 and a strong preference for Chinese-language money movement and guarantee services[4]. CNBC also reported the involvement of Chinese money-laundering networks in moving the Binance proceeds[13].

6Binance's reserves, regulators and political ties

Binance says it has set aside more than $6.34bn of company resources to cover the losses[1,5]. According to CoinDesk, the money comes from its corporate treasury, which held about $5bn in the affected cryptocurrencies, from part of its futures insurance fund and from the Secure Asset Fund for Users (SAFU), an emergency reserve that Binance expects to restore to about $1bn[17]. Replenishment rose from 80% to 90% by 31 October, and Teng put it at about 95% on 1 November[5,17]. Ten tokens, including Bitcoin, Ether, USDT, BNB, Solana, USDC and XRP, were reported fully backed, with the remainder made up of less liquid tokens that take longer to buy[5,17]. Binance published a proof-of-reserves snapshot dated 31 October and said full cryptographic verification would follow by 3 November[5,17].

Markets reacted sharply. The National reported that Bitcoin fell 4.5% to $76,664, Ether 5.2% and Binance's own token BNB 7.1%, with more than $1.5bn of positions liquidated in 24 hours[6]. Al Jazeera reported a six-week low for Bitcoin of $76,368 and a 4.8% fall in shares of Coinbase, a rival exchange that was not affected[12]. Some users on social media cited the collapse of FTX as a reason for doubt[6]. One expert quoted by Al Jazeera observed that assets held this way "are only as safe as the exchange itself"[12].

Binance has covered a hack from its own funds before. In May 2019 it lost 7,000 bitcoins, then worth about $40m, and said SAFU would cover the incident in full; Zhao wrote that "The hackers had the patience to wait, and execute well-orchestrated actions through multiple seemingly independent accounts at the most opportune time"[33]. The present loss is more than 150 times larger in dollar terms.

The exchange's relationship with Washington has also changed. Binance paid a $4.3bn settlement to the US government over allegations that it helped users circumvent federal sanctions, and Zhao pleaded guilty in 2023 to enabling money laundering and served four months in prison[34,35]. Trump pardoned him on 23 October 2025, and the White House press secretary, Karoline Leavitt, said Zhao had been "prosecuted by the Biden Administration in their war on cryptocurrency"[35]. UPI noted earlier Wall Street Journal reporting that a Trump family crypto venture had accumulated $4.5bn with Binance's help[34]. No source has linked those ties to the president's remarks on the hack, but they form part of the political context of the recovery.

Uncertainties and open questions

Several central facts rest on a small number of parties. The size of the theft comes mainly from Binance and TRM Labs, whose figures differ slightly[1,2]. The attribution rests on the FBI's statement and on assessments by TRM Labs and Chainalysis; Binance has not attributed responsibility, Trump has questioned the finding, and North Korea rejected an earlier attribution as "absurd slander"[7,12,20,23]. The technical cause is unsettled: Binance first pointed to a compromised DevOps laptop and later to additional breach points, and Safe Security notes that the exact authorisation pathway remains unverified[14,17]. Reported frozen totals range from about $2.37bn to about $3bn depending on source and timing[2,6,8].

The replenishment percentages and the proof-of-reserves snapshot are Binance's own claims, and the promised full cryptographic verification was not due until 3 November[5,17]. Estimates of North Korea's past thefts differ between Chainalysis and TRM Labs because the firms attribute attacks differently, and the MSMT figures come from a group of governments that excludes Russia and China[9,11,29]. How much of the roughly $3bn not yet frozen can be recovered before it is converted into cash is unknown[13].

Conclusion

The reopening of withdrawals on 1 November ends the most acute phase of the incident for Binance's customers, if not for investigators[1,5]. The exchange says it has replaced about 95% of the stolen assets from its own resources and that users will not bear the loss[1,5]. The FBI, TRM Labs and Chainalysis all point to TraderTraitor, the North Korean activity the FBI previously blamed for the Bybit theft, although Binance has not attributed the attack and the president has voiced doubt[2,3,7,12,18,20]. The roughly $3bn frozen is far larger than the sum frozen after Bybit, but about as much again remains outside investigators' reach and is moving through laundering channels towards eventual cash-out[6,13,32]. For the governments that say such thefts pay for North Korea's weapons of mass destruction and ballistic missile programmes, the open question is whether that remainder can still be stopped[9,13].
