Eight articles of impeachment and the reply from the Pentagon

On Monday 9 November 2026 Thomas Massie, a Republican member of the House of Representatives from Kentucky, introduced on the House floor a privileged resolution containing eight articles of impeachment against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth[1,2]. The 34-page text concludes that Hegseth "warrants impeachment and trial, removal from office, and disqualification to hold and enjoy any office of honor, trust, or profit under the United States"[2]. Its charges accuse Hegseth of waging war on Iran without the authorisation of Congress, disregarding laws meant to limit civilian casualties, ordering extrajudicial killings at sea, seizing Venezuela's president without the approval of Congress and punishing a senator for his speech[1,2,3].

Published summaries group the charges differently. Al Jazeera counts four articles on Iran, two on Venezuela, one on the 2025 bombing campaign in Yemen and one on Hegseth's treatment of Mark Kelly, a Democratic senator from Arizona[1]. Reason counts three articles alleging breaches of the War Powers Resolution over Iran, plus one article each on civilian protection, killings in the Caribbean and Pacific, Kelly, the capture of Nicolás Maduro and Yemen[3].

On Tuesday 10 November Hegseth answered the charges in his first appearance in the Pentagon briefing room since May[4]. He called the effort "a political spectacle, a personal manifesto driven by ego and resentment"[5,6]. "It's a farce, and it's a stunt," he said, adding that no secretary of war or of defence "has ever been impeached for carrying out lawful military operations"[5]. He said every operation had been lawful, ordered by the commander in chief and notified to Congress[5]. "The proper remedy for a policy disagreement is legislation," he argued[1].

Congressional arithmetic and a divided Republican Party

Republicans hold the House by 218 seats to 214, so a few Republican votes added to the Democratic ones could carry the resolution[7]. Massie lost his Republican primary in May and will leave Congress at the end of the year[6,7]. Democrats won control of both the Senate and the House in the midterm elections in early November, and Vice-President JD Vance has named the Iran war, through its effect on energy prices and the cost of living, as one factor in Republican losses[7,8].

Massie has said that "Trump should fire Hegseth to avoid Hegseth's impeachment"[9]. Euronews reported that his aim is to push Trump into replacing Hegseth before Democrats begin their own proceedings[1,10]. After the elections Trump told reporters "I think he's doing a very good job" and said he was not considering replacing him[11]. Other Republicans are less supportive. Speaker Mike Johnson has argued that "Impeaching Hegseth amid hostilities abroad would be a terrible, terrible look"[2]. An Economist/YouGov survey of registered voters, conducted from 18 to 21 September, found 51 per cent supported impeaching Hegseth for starting a war with Iran without the authorisation of Congress and 34 per cent opposed it[12].

Removal is a higher bar. No one may be convicted by the Senate "without the Concurrence of two thirds of the Members present"[13]. In a full Senate, conviction therefore needs 67 votes[1]. Republicans hold 53 Senate seats until the new Congress meets, and Democrats expect 54 next year[1]. Precedents are few. The House impeached Homeland Security Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas by 214 votes to 213 on 13 February 2024, the first impeachment of a cabinet secretary since William Belknap in 1876[14,15].

The Iran war and the War Powers deadline

US military operations against Iran began on 28 February 2026[16,17]. The 1973 War Powers Resolution requires the president to end any use of the armed forces within 60 days of the report he must make to Congress, unless Congress has declared war, specifically authorised the use of force or extended the deadline. He may add up to 30 days if he certifies to Congress that unavoidable military necessity requires it[18].

The House adopted a concurrent resolution, H.Con.Res.86, by 215 votes to 208 on 3 June 2026, and the Senate followed by 50 votes to 48 on 23 June[16,19,20]. A concurrent resolution does not need the president's signature, and the American Society of International Law describes this one as lacking legally binding enforcement power[19]. According to Al Jazeera's summary, the Iran articles accuse Hegseth of ignoring the June resolution and of continuing the war after the 60-day limit expired on 1 May 2026[1].

Civilian deaths and the other charges

The civilian-harm charge centres on a strike on the first day of the war. Amnesty International says Tomahawk missiles struck the Shajareh Tayyebeh elementary school in Minab on 28 February 2026, killing 156 people, including 120 children and 26 teachers[21]. A UN fact-finding mission found reasonable grounds to believe that the United States committed the war crime of launching indiscriminate attacks at Minab[22]. Anna Kelly, the White House deputy press secretary, answered that the Human Rights Council had been "spouting unserious nonsense for decades"[23]. On Yemen, Airwars recorded at least 224 civilian deaths in the 52-day Operation Rough Rider, while the Pentagon's own review acknowledged 153 civilians killed and 243 injured[24,25].

Al Jazeera's tally of US strikes on boats in the Caribbean and eastern Pacific stood at 243 people killed in 69 strikes on 27 October 2026, and US Southern Command says it targets vessels on "established narco-trafficking routes"[26]. Legal scholars and human rights groups describe the campaign as extrajudicial killing[26]. On 3 January 2026 Delta Force soldiers accompanied by FBI agents seized Maduro and his wife in an operation that used more than 150 aircraft[27]. Hegseth calls the operation a "flawless success" completed in 48 minutes[5]. On 12 February Judge Richard Leon blocked Hegseth's action against Kelly, finding that Hegseth had "trampled" on Kelly's First Amendment freedoms[28,29].

What remains uncertain

It is not known how many Republicans will vote for impeachment. Casualty figures conflict. For Minab, Amnesty International gives 156 dead, while NBC News reports that Iranian state media gave 168[21,22]. For Yemen the Pentagon counts 153 civilian deaths and Airwars counts 224[24,25]. The articles, as summarised by Al Jazeera, date the end of the War Powers deadline to 1 May, while Euronews refers to a 90-day limit[1,10]. Hegseth's figures for targets struck, such as more than 13,000 in fewer than 40 days, are his own claims[6]. The polling evidence rests on a single survey, and Truthout's report of it gives no sample size[12].

3A constitutional remedy rarely used against cabinet officers

The Constitution says that "all civil officers of the United States, shall be removed from office on impeachment for, and conviction of, treason, bribery, or other high crimes and misdemeanors"[23]. It gives the House "the sole Power of Impeachment", while the Senate tries the case, and no one may be convicted "without the Concurrence of two thirds of the Members present"[24]. The punishment can go no further than removal from office and disqualification from future office[24]. In a full Senate, conviction therefore needs 67 votes[1]. Republicans hold 53 Senate seats until the new Congress meets, and Democrats expect about 54 next year, so conviction would need substantial Republican support[1]. The Senate Democratic leader, Chuck Schumer, has nonetheless said Hegseth "must stand trial"[1].

There are few precedents. The House unanimously impeached Secretary of War William Belknap on 2 March 1876, a day after he resigned, over more than $40,000 in bribes paid over several years by a trader at Fort Sill. The Senate acquitted him on 1 August after failing to reach a two-thirds majority[25]. On 13 February 2024 the House impeached Homeland Security Secretary Alejandro Mayorkas by 214 votes to 213 over border policy, the first impeachment of a cabinet secretary since Belknap[26]. Kori Schake of the American Enterprise Institute, who argues that Hegseth has damaged the Pentagon, notes that he would be the first civilian Pentagon leader impeached in 150 years[12].

Hegseth has faced impeachment articles before. In December 2025 Representative Shri Thanedar introduced H.Res.935, which charged murder, conspiracy to murder and reckless mishandling of classified information, after reports that a follow-up strike had targeted two survivors of an attack on a boat[27]. Massie's resolution differs in coming from a member of the president's own party.

The central legal question is whether the conduct alleged amounts to "high crimes and misdemeanors". Hegseth calls the charges "policy disagreements, not high crimes". He has warned that impeaching him would lead to soldiers being prosecuted for doing their job, and he compares his position to Trump's two acquittals[1,4]. The articles instead allege that he "knowingly executed and implemented unlawful orders to initiate war against a foreign nation notwithstanding the absence of congressional authorization"[28]. Defense One notes that if this attempt fails, the Democratic majority in the new Congress could revive the proceedings[16].

4The Iran war and the War Powers clock

US military operations against Iran began on 28 February 2026, in a campaign known as Operation Epic Fury[9,29,30]. A ceasefire was agreed in April, and the administration has argued that it met the law's requirements, although fighting continued[10]. The 1973 War Powers Resolution requires the president to end any use of the armed forces within 60 days of the report he must make to Congress, unless Congress has declared war, specifically authorised the use of force or extended the deadline. He may add up to 30 days if he certifies to Congress that unavoidable military necessity requires it[31]. A separate clause says that forces engaged in hostilities without authorisation "shall be removed by the President if the Congress so directs by concurrent resolution"[31].

The House adopted such a resolution, H.Con.Res.86, by 215 votes to 208 on 3 June 2026, with four Republicans in favour, and the Senate followed by 50 votes to 48 on 23 June[9,10,11]. A concurrent resolution does not need the president's signature, and the American Society of International Law describes this one as lacking legally binding enforcement power[10]. The constitutional lawyer Bruce Fein said Trump could ignore it, and Trump said of his own powers, "There are no limits"[9].

Massie's Iran articles rest on this record. According to Al Jazeera's summary, they accuse Hegseth of going to war without congressional approval or an attack on the United States, of ignoring the June resolution, and of continuing the war after the 60-day limit expired on 1 May 2026[1]. Euronews instead describes the charge as fighting beyond a 90-day limit[20]. Ninety days is the most the statute allows once the 30-day extension is added[31].

The Lead Inspector General's report for 28 February to 30 June recorded a Pentagon cost estimate of $33.4 billion as of 29 June, including $22.3 billion in munitions. It also recorded seven service members killed and 417 wounded[29]. Officials told investigators that munitions use had "resulted in strategic inventory shortfalls"[29]. A Congressional Research Service note counts 81 US aircraft lost or damaged and cites Congressional Budget Office estimates of $1.9 billion to $3.3 billion for equipment losses[30]. Hegseth says the campaign hit more than 13,000 targets in under 40 days and destroyed over 80 per cent of Iran's nuclear industrial base[5]. These are the government's own figures and have not been independently checked.

The war has not ended. On 8 November Al Jazeera described an ongoing eight-month conflict[32]. Iran's foreign minister, Abbas Araghchi, demanded an end to hostilities on all fronts and warned that otherwise Iranian forces would "rain down hell". Trump pointed to three aircraft carriers in the region[32].

5Civilian deaths at Minab and in Yemen

The civilian-harm charge centres on a strike on the first day of the war. Amnesty International says Tomahawk missiles struck the Shajareh Tayyebeh elementary school in Minab, in Iran's Hormozgan province, at about 10:45 local time on 28 February 2026. It says the strike killed 156 people, including 120 children and 26 teachers[7]. Amnesty's Evidence Lab examined more than 30 satellite images, 28 videos and 30 photographs and interviewed three people outside Iran. It could not interview witnesses directly because Iranian authorities had shut down the internet[7]. Amnesty concluded that the school had been physically separated from a neighbouring Revolutionary Guards compound since 2016 and that the attack appears to have relied on outdated intelligence[7].

A UN fact-finding mission on Iran, set up by the Human Rights Council in 2022 and chaired by the Bangladeshi jurist Sara Hossain, reported in September on the strike. It found reasonable grounds to believe that the United States committed the war crime of launching indiscriminate attacks at Minab[8,33]. Al Jazeera reported that the finding rested on outdated targeting intelligence and on a facility plainly identifiable as a school in satellite imagery. The human rights lawyer Reed Brody noted that the report has no automatic legal consequences[33]. The same mission documented killings by Iranian state security forces "on a staggering scale"[8]. Anna Kelly, the White House deputy press secretary, answered that the Human Rights Council had been "spouting unserious nonsense for decades"[33]. Massie rejects the idea that the strike was a mistake: "There's no fog of war on the first day"[1].

The Yemen article concerns Operation Rough Rider, a 52-day bombing campaign that ran from March 2025 until 6 May 2025[13]. Hegseth says it struck more than 1,000 targets[5]. The articles say Trump notified Congress 13 days after the strikes began. They also cite a leaked Signal chat in which officials agreed that no imminent threat justified bypassing Congress[1]. Estimates of civilian deaths depend on who counts and how. Airwars, a monitoring group that collects public allegations and checks them against data from the Yemen Data Project and Mwatana for Human Rights, recorded at least 224 civilian deaths. That is close to the 258 it attributes to all US action in Yemen over the previous 23 years[13]. The articles use the figure of 224[1]. The Pentagon's own review, reported in August 2026, acknowledged 153 civilians killed and 243 injured, but its investigators made no site visits and conducted no interviews[14].

6Boat strikes, the Maduro raid and the Kelly case

Since September 2025 the US military has struck boats in the Caribbean and the eastern Pacific that it says were carrying drugs. Al Jazeera's tally on 27 October 2026 stood at 243 people killed in 69 strikes[34]. US Southern Command says it targets vessels on "established narco-trafficking routes" on the basis of intelligence, and the administration has designated criminal groups as terrorist organisations[34]. Relatives of some of the dead say they were fishermen or workers, and legal scholars and human rights groups describe the campaign as extrajudicial killing[34]. One article accuses Hegseth of striking shipwrecked people who posed no threat[1]. In an attack on 2 September 2025, a second strike followed one that killed 11 people. Hegseth said he left after authorising the first strike and that "Adm. Bradley made the correct decision to ultimately sink the boat and eliminate the threat"[35]. Hegseth now says more than 70 drug-carrying vessels have been sunk, while Defense One reports that the strikes have reportedly done little to slow cocaine flows[4,16].

On 3 January 2026 US forces struck targets around Caracas, and Delta Force soldiers accompanied by FBI agents seized Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores, in an operation called Absolute Resolve that used more than 150 aircraft[36]. Trump said the United States would "run" Venezuela until a political transition and would oversee the rebuilding of its oil infrastructure[36]. Maduro had been under indictment in New York since March 2020 on narco-terrorism charges[36]. When arraigned in Manhattan on 5 January, he pleaded not guilty and, as he left the courtroom, told a man who had denounced him, "I am a kidnapped president. I am a prisoner of war"[37]. Massie uses similar language: "It was to kidnap a foreign leader so we could take their oil"[1]. Hegseth calls the operation a "flawless success" completed in 48 minutes[4].

The last article concerns speech. In November 2025 Kelly and other lawmakers released a video telling troops that they could refuse illegal orders[1,38]. Hegseth issued a letter of censure on 5 January and began proceedings that could have reduced Kelly's retired rank and pension, invoking military-law provisions on conduct unbecoming an officer and on prejudicing good order and discipline[38,39]. On 12 February Judge Richard Leon blocked the action, finding that Hegseth had "trampled" on Kelly's First Amendment freedoms[38,39]. The articles accuse Hegseth of placing congressional speech under Defense Department authority[1].

Uncertainties and open questions

Several important facts are unresolved. Published summaries of the 34-page resolution group the charges in different ways, and news outlets quote different portions of its wording[1,2,3]. It is not known how many Republicans will vote for impeachment.

The main casualty figures conflict. For Minab, Amnesty International gives 156 dead, Al Jazeera's account of the UN findings gives 156 to 157, and NBC News reports that Iranian state media gave 168. Amnesty could not interview witnesses inside Iran[7,8,33]. For Yemen, the Pentagon counts 153 civilian deaths and Airwars counts 224. Stars and Stripes cited an Airwars figure of at least 258, but that is the number the group gives for the previous 23 years, not for the 2025 campaign[13,14]. Families dispute the official description of many people killed in the boat strikes[34].

The legal timeline is also uncertain. The articles, as summarised by Al Jazeera, date the end of the War Powers deadline to 1 May, while Euronews refers to a 90-day limit[1,20]. Hegseth's figures for targets struck and damage to Iran's nuclear programme are his own claims[5]. The polling evidence rests on a single Economist/YouGov survey, and Truthout's report of it gives no sample size or margin of error[22].

Conclusion

The House vote will show whether Republican discontent over the Iran war has turned into a willingness to impeach a member of their own administration. Even if the House impeaches Hegseth, removing him needs 67 senators, and Democrats will not have that many in either the current or the incoming Senate[1,24]. The articles still put several questions to Congress as formal charges. The first is whether a war continued after a concurrent resolution under the War Powers Resolution, which the administration treats as non-binding[10,31]. Others concern a school strike that Amnesty International and a UN mission link to the United States, and maritime strikes that legal scholars call extrajudicial[7,8,34]. Hegseth's defence is that these are policy disputes, not high crimes, and that they should be settled through legislation[1,4]. If the attempt fails, the Democratic majority that takes office in January could bring the same charges again[16].
